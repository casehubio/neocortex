# Research Report: Models of Interpersonal Relationships
**Deep Research on Dimensional Decomposition of Relationships**

Date: 2026-10-07
Purpose: Identifying dimensional models for per-relationship perceptions, feelings, and relational patterns to build a cognitive architecture layer for how Character A specifically views and feels about Character B.

---

## Executive Summary

This research identifies **seven distinct dimensional frameworks** for modeling interpersonal relationships beyond global personality:

1. **Interpersonal Circumplex (IPC)** — Agency × Communion axes defining interpersonal behavior patterns
2. **Attachment Theory** — Internal working models creating relationship lenses (Model of Self × Model of Other)
3. **Object Relations Theory** — Internal representations of self-and-other dyads, particularly splitting in pathology
4. **Sternberg's Triangular Theory** — Intimacy × Passion × Commitment (applicable beyond romance)
5. **McCroskey & McCain's Interpersonal Attraction** — Social × Physical × Task attraction (three independent dimensions)
6. **Baldwin's Relational Schemas** — Relationship-specific {self-schema, other-schema, interpersonal script} triads
7. **Perceived Partner Responsiveness** — Understanding × Validation × Care dimensions

**Key finding for cognitive architecture:** Personality disorders systematically distort per-relationship perceptions through specific biases (BPD: hypermentalizing + rejection sensitivity; NPD: self-focus + trustworthiness underestimation; ASPD: mentalizing deficits + hostile attribution bias).

## 1. Interpersonal Circumplex (IPC)

### Core Model

The interpersonal circumplex is a mathematically defined circular model organizing interpersonal dispositions around **two orthogonal dimensions**:

- **Agency (Vertical Axis)**: Dominance ↔ Submission / Power ↔ Meekness / Assertiveness ↔ Passivity
- **Communion (Horizontal Axis)**: Warmth ↔ Coldness / Friendliness ↔ Hostility / Affiliation ↔ Distance

### Eight Octants (Standard Labels)

The continuous space is divided into eight segments, coded PA through NO:

| Octant | Trait Label | Problem Label | Dimensional Composition |
|--------|-------------|---------------|------------------------|
| PA | Assured-Dominant | Domineering | High Agency |
| BC | Arrogant-Calculating | Selfish/Vindictive | High Agency + Low Communion |
| DE | Coldhearted | Distant/Cold | Low Communion |
| FG | Aloof-Introverted | Socially Inhibited | Low Agency + Low Communion |
| HI | Unassured-Submissive | Nonassertive | Low Agency |
| JK | Unassuming-Ingenuous | Exploitable/Overly Accommodating | Low Agency + High Communion |
| LM | Warm-Agreeable | Self-Sacrificing | High Communion |
| NO | Gregarious-Extraverted | Intrusive | High Agency + High Communion |

### Structural Predictions

The circumplex makes **quantitative predictions** about correlations:
- **Adjacent octants** (45° apart): Positive correlation
- **Opposite octants** (180° apart): Negative correlation
- **Orthogonal octants** (90° apart): Zero correlation

### Complementarity in Relationships

**Complementarity principle**: Behaviors along axes pull for specific partner responses:

- **Communion (correspondence)**: Warmth invites warmth; hostility invites hostility
- **Agency (reciprocity)**: Dominance invites submission; submission invites dominance

**Empirical findings**:
- Complementary dyads (dominant-submissive pairings) show higher relationship satisfaction than similar pairings
- Complementary dyads engage in more verbal exchanges and prefer less interpersonal distance than anticomplementary ones
- Individual complementarity coefficients predict being liked by interaction partners and observer judgments of social competence

### Application to Cluster B Personality Disorders

**Narcissistic Personality Disorder (NPD)**:
- Primary location: PA octant (Assured-Dominant)
- High on Agency dimension, variable on Communion
- Self-ratings tend toward Quadrant I (friendly-dominant)
- Others' ratings often show BC octant (Arrogant-Calculating) — high agency, low communion

**Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD)**:
- Primary location: HI octant (Unassured-Submissive)
- Reflects dependency, fear of abandonment
- Characterized by interpersonal problems including nonassertiveness and submission
- Shows instability across octants due to splitting (can shift rapidly between idealization and devaluation)

**Histrionic Personality Disorder (HPD)**:
- Characterized by attention-seeking (high agency) and emotional expression (high communion attempts)
- Likely NO octant (Intrusive) — high agency + high communion orientation
- Relationships marked by shallow emotional expression and excessive attention demands

**Antisocial Personality Disorder (ASPD)**:
- BC octant (Arrogant-Calculating) or PA/BC boundary
- High agency, low communion
- Cold, vindictive, hostile interpersonal style
- Exploitative and dominating relational patterns

## 2. Attachment Theory: Internal Working Models

### Bowlby's Framework (1969)

**Internal Working Model (IWM)**: Mental representation of:
1. Whether attachment figures are available and responsive
2. Whether the self is worthy of care and love

**Function**: The IWM serves as a **perceptual filter** through which all subsequent relational data is processed, creating self-fulfilling prophecies in relational patterns.

### Bartholomew & Horowitz's Four-Category Model (1991)

Two orthogonal dimensions create four attachment styles:

**Dimensions**:
- **Model of Self**: Positive (worthy of love) ↔ Negative (unworthy)
- **Model of Other**: Positive (trustworthy, available) ↔ Negative (rejecting, unavailable)

**Four Attachment Styles**:

| Style | Model of Self | Model of Other | Characteristics |
|-------|--------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Secure** | Positive | Positive | Comfortable with intimacy and autonomy; trusts partners; handles conflict constructively |
| **Anxious-Preoccupied** | Negative | Positive | Intense need for reassurance; fears abandonment; hypervigilant to relationship threats; proximity-seeking |
| **Dismissive-Avoidant** | Positive | Negative | Values independence; uncomfortable with intimacy; suppresses attachment needs; expects hurt |
| **Fearful-Avoidant** | Negative | Negative | Wants intimacy but fears rejection; conflicting representations; most unstable pattern |

### How Attachment Styles Filter Interpersonal Perception

**Anxious attachment**:
- More likely to perceive others as anxious (projection)
- See more distress in facial expressions
- Interpret ambiguous social cues as threatening
- Hypervigilant to rejection signals

**Avoidant attachment**:
- Do NOT show bias toward seeing others as avoidant
- May actually be MORE accurate at identifying others' attachment patterns at first glance
- Minimize emotional significance of relationships

**General principle**: Working models of attachment function as knowledge structures through which social events are filtered and understood. Adults with different working models interpret relationship events in ways consistent with their existing self-and-other models.

### Link to Personality Disorders

**BPD and attachment**:
- Often fearful-avoidant attachment (negative self + negative other)
- Intense fear of abandonment with simultaneous fear of intimacy
- Attachment system activation triggers mentalizing deficits

**NPD and attachment**:
- Often dismissive-avoidant (positive self + negative other)
- Discomfort with emotional closeness
- Devaluation of others protects grandiose self

**ASPD/Psychopathy and attachment**:
- Predominantly avoidant attachment patterns
- Unable to form close intimate bonds
- Behavioral features (Factor 2) associated with both anxious and avoidant insecurity

## 3. Object Relations Theory

### Kernberg's Framework

**Core concept**: Experience is organized around **internal object relations dyads** consisting of:
1. A self-representation
2. An object-representation (the other)
3. An affect linking them

These dyads are **internalized relationship units** that structure all subsequent interpersonal experience.

### Splitting Defense Mechanism

**Splitting**: Active dissociation of contradictory self/other experiences to manage anxiety

**Normal development**: Infant initially experiences caregivers as entirely good or entirely bad (paranoid-schizoid position, Klein), then integrates these into whole-object representations (depressive position)

**Pathological splitting**: Failure to integrate good and bad representations → persistent all-good vs. all-bad object representations in adulthood

### Borderline Personality Organization (Kernberg)

**Core pathology**: Identity diffusion based on split self- and other-representations

**Characteristics**:
- Alternation between idealized and persecutory object representations
- Others experienced as either perfect saviors or malevolent persecutors
- Rapid shifts: "greatest partner ever" → "worst partner in the world"
- No stable, integrated middle ground

**Primitive defenses** (Kernberg):
- Splitting
- Projective identification
- Primitive idealization and devaluation
- Omnipotence and omnipotent control
- Denial

**Clinical significance**: BPD diagnostic criterion — "pattern of unstable and intense interpersonal relationships characterized by alternating between extremes of idealization and devaluation"

### Narcissistic Personality Disorder (Kernberg)

**Pathological grandiose self**: Fusion of ideal self + ideal object + actual self representations

**Function**: Defends against underlying worthlessness and rage

**Cost**: Prevents genuine intimacy and emotional depth; others are not seen as separate whole persons but as extensions of the grandiose self or threats to it

**Contrast with Kohut**: Kernberg emphasized aggression and pathological object relations; Kohut emphasized developmental arrest and unmet empathic needs

### Key Theorists

**Melanie Klein**:
- Paranoid-schizoid position (0-6 months): splitting into all-good/all-bad
- Depressive position (6+ months): integration of good/bad into whole objects
- Defenses: introjection, projection, splitting, fantasy

**Donald Winnicott**:
- "Good enough mother" concept
- True self vs. false self
- Transitional objects and space

**Otto Kernberg**:
- Borderline personality organization (BPO) concept
- Transference-focused psychotherapy (TFP)
- Object relations model of severe personality disorders

## 4. Sternberg's Triangular Theory of Love (1986)

### Three Components

**1. Intimacy**:
- Feelings of closeness, connectedness, bondedness
- Emotional warmth in the relationship
- Understanding, sharing, communication
- Giving and receiving emotional support

**2. Passion**:
- Drives leading to romance, physical attraction, sexual consummation
- Motivational component
- Arousal, excitement, desire
- The "hot" component

**3. Decision/Commitment**:
- Short-term: Decision that one loves another
- Long-term: Commitment to maintain that love
- The "cold" cognitive component
- Loyalty, dedication, maintenance behaviors

### Eight Types of Love

Combinations of the three components create eight distinct types:

| Type | Intimacy | Passion | Commitment | Description |
|------|----------|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Non-love** | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | Absence of all components |
| **Liking/Friendship** | ✓ | ✗ | ✗ | True friendships without passion or long-term commitment |
| **Infatuation** | ✗ | ✓ | ✗ | "Love at first sight" — passion alone |
| **Empty Love** | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ | Commitment without intimacy or passion |
| **Romantic Love** | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ | Liking + physical attraction, but not committed |
| **Companionate Love** | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ | Long-term friendship, family bonds, passionless marriages |
| **Fatuous Love** | ✗ | ✓ | ✓ | Whirlwind romance with commitment but no deep connection |
| **Consummate Love** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | "Complete" love — the ideal |

### Application Beyond Romantic Relationships

**Friendship**:
- Intimacy (closeness) is central
- "Passion" reinterpreted as **enthusiasm** or excitement about the relationship
- Commitment as loyalty, reliability, willingness to sacrifice for the friend

**Family bonds**:
- Companionate love (intimacy + commitment) is typical
- Long-term, deep bonds without romantic passion

**Loyalty and protective bonds**:
- Commitment component is central
- Could model protective relationships (mentor-protégé, guardian-ward)

**Empirical applications**:
- Researchers have adapted the Triangular Theory to study friendships
- Used in marketing research as "brand love"
- Successfully applied to non-romantic close relationships across cultures

**Conclusion**: While originally designed for romantic love, the three dimensions (reinterpreted as closeness, enthusiasm, and loyalty) can model diverse relationship types including mentorship, protective bonds, and alliance relationships.

## 5. McCroskey & McCain's Interpersonal Attraction (1974)

### Three Independent Dimensions

Factor analysis of 30 Likert-type scales (215 subjects) revealed **three orthogonal dimensions**:

**1. Social Attraction**:
- Desire for friendship and social interaction
- Wanting to spend time together socially
- "I think he/she could be a friend of mine"
- Internal reliability: α = .86

**2. Physical Attraction**:
- Aesthetic and sexual desirability
- Appearance-based draw
- "I think he/she is quite handsome/pretty"
- Internal reliability: α = .80

**3. Task Attraction**:
- Wanting to work together
- Competence-based respect
- Perceived utility for collaborative work
- "I have confidence in his/her ability to get the job done"
- Internal reliability: α = .75

### Key Properties

**Independence**: The three dimensions are **orthogonal** — you can be high on one and low on another:
- High task attraction + low social attraction = "competent but I don't want to hang out with them"
- High physical attraction + low task attraction = "attractive but I wouldn't work with them"
- High social attraction + low physical attraction = "great friend, not attracted romantically"

**Measurement**: 15-item instrument (5 items per dimension) with seven-step Likert scales

**Replication**: Results replicated across four additional studies, demonstrating robustness

### Application to Character Relationships

This model is particularly useful for **non-romantic relationships** where different forms of attraction operate independently:

- **Alliance partners**: High task attraction, variable social attraction
- **Rivals**: Potentially high task attraction (respect for competence) with negative social attraction
- **Mentorship**: High task attraction + moderate social attraction
- **Pure friendship**: High social attraction, low task/physical focus

**Critical insight**: Interpersonal attraction is **multidimensional**, not a single "liking" dimension. Characters can simultaneously respect someone's competence, find them physically unattractive, and have no desire to socialize with them.

## 6. Baldwin's Relational Schemas (1992)

### Core Framework

**Relational schema** = Cognitive structure representing regularities in patterns of interpersonal relatedness

**Three interdependent components**:

1. **Self-schema**: How the self is experienced in this specific interpersonal situation
2. **Other-schema**: Schema for the other person in the interaction
3. **Interpersonal script**: Sequence of actions and events defining the stereotyped relational pattern

### Key Insight: Relationship Specificity

**Critical point**: People develop **different cognitive representations for different relationships**, each containing distinct if-then expectations.

**Example**: 
- With Person A: Self-schema = "competent", Other-schema = "supportive mentor", Script = "I ask questions, they guide me"
- With Person B: Self-schema = "defensive", Other-schema = "critical judge", Script = "I anticipate criticism, they find flaws"

The **same person** experiences themselves differently across relationships based on the activated relational schema.

### Integration of Components

Baldwin (1992) demonstrated that the three subsets of knowledge are so **highly interdependent** that any change in one affects the others — they function as a unified, abstract cognitive schema.

**Activation**: Relational schemas are activated by:
- Situational cues (seeing the person, anticipating interaction)
- Priming (reminders of similar past relationships)
- Current emotional state

### Link to Personality Disorders: Early Maladaptive Schemas

**Schema Therapy** (Young): Extended Baldwin's framework to understand personality pathology

**Early Maladaptive Schemas (EMS)**: Pervasive, dysfunctional themes developed in childhood from:
- Unmet core emotional needs
- Adverse childhood events
- Toxic parenting patterns

**18 Early Maladaptive Schemas** organized into five domains:
1. **Disconnection/Rejection**: Abandonment, mistrust/abuse, emotional deprivation, defectiveness/shame, social isolation
2. **Impaired Autonomy**: Dependence/incompetence, vulnerability to harm, enmeshment, failure
3. **Impaired Limits**: Entitlement/grandiosity, insufficient self-control
4. **Other-Directedness**: Subjugation, self-sacrifice, approval-seeking
5. **Overvigilance/Inhibition**: Negativity/pessimism, emotional inhibition, unrelenting standards, punitiveness

### EMS Patterns by Personality Disorder

**Borderline Personality Disorder**:
- Highest on: Abandonment, Defectiveness/Shame, Dependence/Incompetence
- Domain: Disconnection/Rejection schemas most prevalent
- Effect: Hypervigilance to abandonment cues in all relationships

**Narcissistic Personality Disorder**:
- Entitlement/Grandiosity schema
- Defectiveness/Shame (defended against via grandiosity)
- Effect: All relationships filtered through superiority/inferiority lens

**Antisocial Personality Disorder**:
- Mistrust/Abuse schema
- Entitlement
- Insufficient Self-Control
- Effect: Others seen primarily as threats or tools

**General principle**: Pathological relational schemas create **template-driven** rather than **reality-responsive** relationship perceptions. The person imposes the same dysfunctional relational pattern across diverse relationships.

## 7. Per-Relationship Emotional Dimensions

### Perceived Partner Responsiveness (PPR)

**Definition**: The felt sense that a relationship partner understands who you are, validates the importance of your experience, and cares about your well-being.

**Three core dimensions**:

**1. Understanding**:
- Perception that partner accurately comprehends the self
- Includes feelings, beliefs, goals, history, self-perceptions
- "They really get me"

**2. Validation**:
- Perception that partner sees one's inner experience as legitimate and valuable
- "My feelings/thoughts matter to them"
- Affirmation of worth

**3. Care**:
- Perception that partner is invested in one's well-being
- Active concern and support
- "They have my back"

**Importance**: PPR is a **fundamental construct** in development and maintenance of intimacy. It predicts relationship satisfaction, stability, and mental health outcomes.

**Measurement**: 12-item Perceived Partner Responsiveness Scale (PPRS) with subscales for each dimension

### Relationship Quality Dimensions (Beyond PPR)

**Trust**:
- Two facets: Trust in honesty + Trust in benevolence
- Central predictor of relationship quality
- Better predictor of satisfaction than liking or loving in some studies

**Respect**:
- Recognition of inherent worth, individuality, autonomy
- Better predictor of relationship satisfaction than liking/loving/attachment in one study
- Ensures relationships built on equality rather than power/control

**Commitment**:
- Intention to persist in the relationship
- Psychological attachment to the partner
- Long-term orientation

**Satisfaction**:
- Overall evaluation of relationship quality
- Affective appraisal

**Affective Conflict**:
- Emotional tension and discord in the relationship
- Negative dimension

### Relationship-Specific Emotions

**Social comparison emotions**:

**Envy** (two types):
- **Benign envy**: Experienced when upward comparison is deserved and controllable → motivates self-improvement
- **Malicious envy**: Experienced when upward comparison is undeserved → motivates harm to other

**Admiration**:
- Positive response to upward comparison
- Contains explicit social comparison in some definitions
- Motivates approach and emulation

**Resentment**:
- Directed at perceived undeserved advantage
- Scholars debate: Is it malicious envy, or anger at higher-status individuals?

**Contempt**:
- Directed at lower-status individuals (per some philosophers)
- Fundamental negative emotion in relationships
- Predicts relationship dissolution

**Gratitude**:
- Response to perceived kindness/benefit
- Strengthens relational bonds

**Status-based emotions**:
- **Resentment** → toward higher-status
- **Anger** → toward equal-status
- **Contempt** → toward lower-status
(Per one philosophical taxonomy)

### Partner Perception Biases

**Idealization**:
- Attributing exaggeratedly positive qualities to partner
- **Adaptive** in moderate amounts — predicts relationship satisfaction and stability
- "Positive illusions" help sustain relationships

**Accuracy**:
- Understanding partner's true characteristics
- Can coexist with bias: "accurate by being biased"
- Partners who know each other well can be both accurate AND idealize

**Motivated cognition**:
- Selective attention to information confirming desired conclusions
- Biased memory for relationship events
- Charitable interpretations of ambiguous partner behavior

**Meta-perceptions of bias**:
- People accurately perceive when they are biased
- "I know I'm seeing them through rose-colored glasses"
- Anchored to actual levels of bias

**Underestimation effects**:
- Underestimating partner's positive qualities → lower actor satisfaction
- BUT higher partner satisfaction (suggests humility/gratitude)

## 8. How Personality Disorders Distort Relational Perception

### Borderline Personality Disorder (BPD)

**Primary distortions**:

**1. Rejection Sensitivity**:
- Hypervigilance to signs of abandonment
- Misinterpret ambiguous social cues as rejection
- Perceive rejection more frequently than controls
- Perceive acceptance less frequently

**2. Negative Emotion Recognition Bias**:
- Interpret neutral or ambiguous facial expressions as negative
- See anger or fear where none was intended
- Well-documented, robust finding in BPD research

**3. Hypermentalizing**:
- OVER-attribution of mental states to others
- Excessive inference about others' thoughts/feelings
- When BPD individuals make ToM errors, they are **overmentalizing errors**
- Contrasts with other disorders (ASPD shows undermentalizing)

**4. Trust Appraisal Deficits**:
- Lower ratings of facial trustworthiness
- Mediated by rejection sensitivity
- Even neutral faces seen as less trustworthy

**5. Fluctuating Mentalizing**:
- Can mentalize accurately in some situations
- "Lose" mentalizing ability when attachment system activated
- Stress/emotional arousal → mentalizing collapse

**Neural correlates**:
- Heightened amygdala response to negative emotional stimuli
- Greater dorsal anterior cingulate cortex activity to social rejection
- Greater medial prefrontal cortex activity to social rejection

**Relational impact**: BPD creates a **negativity filter** over relationship perceptions, with ambiguous cues systematically interpreted as threatening/rejecting, self-fulfilling the fear of abandonment.

### Narcissistic Personality Disorder (NPD)

**Primary distortions**:

**1. Reduced Trust of Others**:
- Antagonistic narcissism negatively related to perceiving others as trustful
- Less trust toward strangers, especially after receiving feedback
- Defensive mistrust protects grandiose self

**2. Self-Focused Attention**:
- Greater concentration on own mental states
- Decreased sense of others
- Others perceived primarily through self-relevance lens

**3. Interpretation of Ambiguous Cues as Rejection**:
- High narcissistic traits → interpret ambiguous social cues as rejection
- Similar to BPD, but mechanism differs (grandiosity threat vs. abandonment fear)

**4. Perception-Reality Gap**:
- Narcissists understand others see them less positively than they see themselves (meta-accuracy)
- Know they make positive first impressions that deteriorate over time
- Have insight into their narcissistic personality
- YET maintain grandiose self despite this awareness

**5. Cognitive Biases**:
- **Perceptual**: Less holistic, more analytical perception
- **Attentional**: Bias toward positive, self-referential cues
- **Memory**: Biased memory for self-relevant information
- Self-focused orientation creates distortions in perceiving, interpreting, and remembering information about others

**6. Projection**:
- Project onto others what they think of themselves
- Expect others perceive them as they perceive themselves
- Transparency illusion: "I am a great open book"

**Relational impact**: NPD creates a **self-reference filter** where others are perceived primarily in relation to maintaining the grandiose self. Others are not seen as separate whole persons with independent value.

### Antisocial Personality Disorder (ASPD) / Psychopathy

**Primary distortions**:

**1. Empathy Deficits**:
- Limited ability to empathize regardless of psychopathic traits
- Deficits in both cognitive empathy (ToM) and affective empathy
- Meta-analysis: robust link between ASPD and deficits in recognizing fearful expressions

**2. Mentalizing Problems**:
- Poor general mentalizing capacities
- Specific problems in emotion recognition
- Reduced emotional reactivity
- 19% error rate in self-other distinction tasks (vs. 4% in controls)

**3. Hostile Attribution Bias**:
- Tendency to interpret neutral situations as potentially hostile
- Related to increased anger and threat perception
- Reduced experienced trust in social interaction
- Inability to properly perceive/attribute emotions → bias toward hostility

**4. Focus on Others for Manipulation**:
- ASPD shows reduced understanding of self
- Better at interpreting others (for instrumental purposes)
- Concentration on manipulating others
- Others categorized by utility: target, tool, obstacle, threat

**5. Cold, Vindictive Interpersonal Style**:
- Lack motivation to engage empathically
- Domination and exploitativeness
- Callousness and cruelty
- Emotional insensitivity

**6. Attachment Patterns**:
- Predominantly avoidant attachment
- Unable to form close intimate bonds
- Relationships characterized by fear of rejection, boundary problems, demanding behavior (Factor 2)

**Relational impact**: ASPD creates a **utility filter** where others are perceived primarily through instrumental value. Relationships lack genuine emotional bonds and are organized around dominance and exploitation.

### Histrionic Personality Disorder (HPD)

**Primary distortions**:

**1. Attention-Focused Perception**:
- Others perceived primarily as audience members
- Uncomfortable when not center of attention
- Others' value determined by attention-giving capacity

**2. Emotional Recognition Deficits**:
- Decreased ability to recognize others' emotions
- Own emotions appear superficial or exaggerated to others
- Rapidly shifting emotions that don't track with situational reality

**3. Shallow Relational Processing**:
- Lack genuine emotional intimacy
- Relationships lack depth despite apparent emotional intensity
- Superficial engagement masking absence of true connection

**Relational impact**: HPD creates an **attention filter** where others are perceived primarily as sources of validation and audience for emotional performance.

### Comparative Mentalizing Profiles

| Disorder | Self-Understanding | Other-Understanding | Error Pattern | Empathy |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------|---------------|---------|
| **BPD** | Fluctuating | Fluctuating | Hypermentalizing | High affective, low cognitive |
| **NPD** | Inflated/defensive | Self-focused lens | Rejection-biased | Low overall |
| **ASPD** | Reduced | Instrumental | Undermentalizing + hostile bias | Very low affective and cognitive |
| **HPD** | Shallow/shifting | Attention-focused | Emotion recognition deficits | Superficial |

## Synthesis: Dimensional Architecture for Per-Relationship Modeling

### Layer 1: Trait-Level Interpersonal Style (IPC)

**What it models**: Character A's **general interpersonal behavior pattern** across relationships

**Dimensions**:
- Agency (Dominance ↔ Submission)
- Communion (Warmth ↔ Coldness)

**Application**: Provides baseline octant (PA, BC, DE, FG, HI, JK, LM, NO) for character's typical interpersonal stance

### Layer 2: Relational Lens (Attachment + Object Relations)

**What it models**: Character A's **perceptual filter** carried into all relationships

**Dimensions**:
- Model of Self (Worthy ↔ Unworthy)
- Model of Other (Trustworthy ↔ Rejecting)
- Object representations (Integrated ↔ Split into all-good/all-bad)

**Application**: 
- Determines default expectations about relationships
- In pathology: Creates systematic distortions (BPD splits, NPD grandiosity, ASPD mistrust)

### Layer 3: Relationship-Specific Schema (Baldwin)

**What it models**: Character A's representation of **this specific relationship** with Character B

**Components**:
- Self-in-this-relationship schema
- Other-in-this-relationship schema
- Interpersonal script (if-then expectations)

**Application**: Same character experiences self differently with different others; different behavioral patterns activated per relationship

### Layer 4: Relationship Type and Intensity (Sternberg)

**What it models**: **What kind of bond** exists between A and B

**Dimensions**:
- Intimacy (Closeness, emotional connection)
- Passion (Enthusiasm, excitement about relationship — not only romantic)
- Commitment (Loyalty, intention to maintain)

**Eight types**: Non-love, Friendship, Infatuation, Empty love, Romantic love, Companionate love, Fatuous love, Consummate love

**Application**: Determines relationship category; applicable to romance, friendship, family, mentorship, alliance

### Layer 5: Attraction Profile (McCroskey & McCain)

**What it models**: **Why** Character A is drawn to (or repelled by) Character B

**Three independent dimensions**:
- Social Attraction (want to be friends, socialize)
- Physical Attraction (appearance-based draw, aesthetic/sexual)
- Task Attraction (want to work together, respect competence)

**Application**: 
- Models orthogonal forms of attraction
- Allows "I respect your competence but don't like you socially"
- Critical for non-romantic relationships

### Layer 6: Perceived Partner Responsiveness

**What it models**: Character A's perception of **how Character B treats them**

**Dimensions**:
- Understanding ("They get me")
- Validation ("They think I matter")
- Care ("They want good things for me")

**Application**: Predicts relationship satisfaction, intimacy, and stability

### Layer 7: Relationship-Specific Emotions

**What it models**: **How Character A feels toward Character B**

**Emotions to model**:
- Trust vs. Mistrust
- Respect vs. Contempt
- Gratitude vs. Resentment
- Admiration vs. Envy (benign vs. malicious)
- Comfort vs. Fear
- Safety vs. Threat

**Status-based emotions**:
- Upward comparison: Admiration, Envy, Resentment
- Lateral comparison: Jealousy, Rivalry
- Downward comparison: Contempt, Pity

**Application**: These are **per-relationship states**, not global personality traits

### Layer 8: Personality Disorder Filters (When Applicable)

**What it models**: **Systematic distortions** in how Character A perceives Character B

**BPD filter**:
- Hypermentalizing (over-infer mental states)
- Rejection sensitivity (ambiguous → threatening)
- Negative emotion bias (neutral faces → angry/fearful)
- Splitting (all-good ↔ all-bad, unstable)

**NPD filter**:
- Self-reference bias (others perceived through self-relevance)
- Reduced trust
- Grandiosity maintenance (others as admirers or threats)

**ASPD filter**:
- Hostile attribution bias (neutral → hostile)
- Mentalizing deficits (poor emotion recognition)
- Utility categorization (target/tool/obstacle/threat)
- Callousness (low emotional resonance)

**Application**: In pathological characters, these filters create predictable, maladaptive relationship patterns

## Recommendations for Cognitive Architecture Implementation

### 1. **Start with Relationship Schema as Core Unit**

Use Baldwin's framework as the **primary data structure**:

```
RelationshipSchema(A → B) = {
  self_schema: SelfConcept,           // How A experiences self with B
  other_schema: OtherConcept,         // How A represents B
  interpersonal_script: Script,        // A's if-then expectations with B
  
  attraction_profile: {                // McCroskey & McCain
    social: float,
    physical: float,
    task: float
  },
  
  bond_type: {                         // Sternberg
    intimacy: float,
    passion: float,
    commitment: float
  },
  
  perceived_responsiveness: {          // PPR
    understanding: float,
    validation: float,
    care: float
  },
```

### 3. **Implement IPC as Behavioral Tendencies with Situational Modulation**

IPC octant is a **baseline**, modulated by relationship context:

```
get_interpersonal_behavior(actor, target, situation):
  baseline_octant = actor.ipc_octant
  relationship = actor.relationships[target]
  
  # Modulate by relationship-specific factors
  agency_mod = f(relationship.perceived_responsiveness.validation,
                  relationship.emotional_state.safety,
                  actor.attachment_style.anxiety)
  
  communion_mod = f(relationship.attraction_profile.social,
                     relationship.emotional_state.trust,
                     actor.object_relations.current_valence[target])
  
  return modulate_octant(baseline_octant, agency_mod, communion_mod)
```

### 4. **Model PD Filters as Perceptual Transformations**

Personality disorder distortions are **input transformations**:

```
perceive_other_behavior(actor, target, behavior):
  raw_perception = behavior

    raw_perception = apply_rejection_sensitivity(raw_perception)

  if actor.has_disorder(NPD):
```

### 5. **Use Complementarity to Predict Relationship Dynamics**

IPC complementarity predicts interaction satisfaction:

```
compute_interaction_quality(actor, target):
  actor_agency = actor.ipc_position.agency
  actor_communion = actor.ipc_position.communion
  target_agency = target.ipc_position.agency
  target_communion = target.ipc_position.communion
  
  # Communion should correspond (match)
  communion_fit = 1.0 - abs(actor_communion - target_communion)
  
  # Agency should reciprocate (opposite)
  agency_fit = 1.0 - abs(actor_agency + target_agency)  # Want sum near 0
  
  complementarity = (communion_fit + agency_fit) / 2.0
  
  return complementarity  # Higher = more satisfying interaction
```

### 6. **Track Relationship Evolution via Schema Updates**

Relational schemas update based on experience:

```
update_relationship_schema(actor, target, interaction):
  schema = actor.relationships[target]
  
  # Update perceived responsiveness based on interaction
  schema.perceived_responsiveness.understanding += 
    delta_understanding(interaction)
  schema.perceived_responsiveness.validation += 
    delta_validation(interaction)
  schema.perceived_responsiveness.care += 
    delta_care(interaction)
  
  # Update emotional state
  if interaction.was_betrayal:
    schema.emotional_state.trust -= TRUST_DECAY
    schema.emotional_state.resentment += RESENTMENT_GAIN
  
  # Update bond type
  if interaction.was_intimate:
    schema.bond_type.intimacy += INTIMACY_GAIN
  
  # In BPD: Trigger splitting if threshold crossed
  if actor.has_disorder(BPD):
    if schema.emotional_state.trust < actor.object_relations.splitting_threshold:
      schema.other_schema = "ALL_BAD"  # Idealization → Devaluation
```

### 7. **Differentiate Global Traits from Relationship-Specific States**

Critical distinction:

**Global (Character-level)**:
- IPC baseline octant
- Attachment style
- Personality disorder filters
- General drives (from existing architecture)

**Relationship-specific (Per dyad)**:
- Attraction profile (social/physical/task)
- Bond type (intimacy/passion/commitment)
- Perceived responsiveness
- Trust, respect, admiration, envy toward this specific person
- Self-schema in this relationship
- Other-schema for this person
- Interpersonal script with this person

**Error to avoid**: Don't model "trust" as a character trait. Model it as a per-relationship state with a character-level prior (from attachment style).
